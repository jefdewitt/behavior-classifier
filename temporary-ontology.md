# Temporary Ontology — v0.1

## 1. Criticism

**Operational definition:**
A statement that assigns a negative trait, motive, or character judgment to the other person rather than describing a specific behavior or problem. Criticism often generalizes from an event into a claim about **who the person is**, especially through language like “you always,” “you never,” “you’re selfish,” or “you don’t care.”

**Positive examples:**

* “You never listen to me. Everything is always about you.”
* “You’re so irresponsible. I can’t count on you for anything.”

**Negative examples:**

* “I was frustrated that you didn’t call when you said you would.”
* “Can you put the dishes away tonight? I’ve done them the last three nights.”

---

## 2. Defensiveness

**Operational definition:**
A response that primarily rejects, excuses, counterattacks, or redirects responsibility after a complaint, concern, or request. The defining feature is **resistance to acknowledging one’s own possible contribution**, rather than merely disagreeing with the other person’s interpretation.

**Positive examples:**

* “That’s not my fault. You’re the one who made us late.”
* “Why are you coming after me? You do the exact same thing.”

**Negative examples:**

* “I don’t completely agree with how you see it, but I can see why what I did upset you.”
* “Yeah, I forgot. That was on me.”

---

## 3. Validation

**Operational definition:**
A statement that explicitly communicates that the other person’s feeling, perspective, or reaction is understandable, recognizable, or meaningful—even if the speaker does not fully agree with the underlying conclusion. Validation acknowledges the other person’s **subjective experience without requiring agreement on the facts**.

**Positive examples:**

* “I can understand why you felt ignored when I kept looking at my phone.”
* “That makes sense. I’d probably be frustrated too if I thought you weren’t hearing me.”

**Negative examples:**

* “You’re overreacting. It wasn’t a big deal.”
* “Okay, fine, whatever you say.”

---

## 4. Repair Attempt

**Operational definition:**
A statement or conversational move intended to interrupt conflict, reduce hostility, restore connection, or make continued discussion safer and more productive. Repair attempts can include apologies, taking responsibility, softening, expressing affection, requesting a reset, acknowledging escalation, or proposing a constructive way forward.

**Positive examples:**

* “I’m getting defensive, and I don’t want this to turn into a fight. Can I start that again?”
* “I’m sorry. That came out much harsher than I meant it. I want to understand what you’re saying.”

**Negative examples:**

* “Whatever. Can we just stop talking about this?”
* “Fine, I’m sorry you feel that way.”

---

## Annotation rule of thumb

Label the **observable conversational behavior**, not the presumed emotion, personality, diagnosis, or intent.

For the POC, I’d also allow **multiple labels on the same utterance**. For example:

> “Yeah, I forgot, and I’m sorry. I can see why you’re pissed.”

could reasonably contain both **repair attempt** and **validation**.

Likewise:

> “Maybe I forgot because you’re constantly nagging me.”

could contain both **defensiveness** and **criticism**.

That keeps the ontology much closer to how actual arguments work than forcing every utterance into exactly one bucket.
